# Pickles — curated head-scratchers

A **pickle** is a single Snag Poker decision with no obvious answer: a hand that
could swing either way, to ponder on the commute. Curated by hand, never random
nonsense. After the player chooses, Willie reveals what the odds said for each
option: **win the pot**, **shed snags**, and **snag risk** (taking the
opponent's snags if you lose).

---

## Pickle #1 — "Nines or the Straight?"

*From a real tournament, crossing 67, against Gaylord Ravenal.*

**The situation (on the Turn)**
- Your hand: **9♦ 7♠ 5♦**
- Board: **10♣ 9♠ 3♣ 4♣**
- You already threw away the A♠ and 6♡ on the Flop.
- Gaylord threw away the K♣ on the Flop and kept two cards.
- You carry **5 snags**; no snags are left in the deck (draws are safe).
- Pot: $104 (you $53, Gaylord $51). Late game: the River fee is $11, your next
  discards cost $3 and $4.

**The question:** stand pat, or throw the 7 and 5?

**Option A — stand pat ($11)**
- Keeps the **pair of Nines** *and* the straight draw: 3-4-5-6-7 needs only a 6
  (three left), and it does not use the 9.
- River helps with a 6 (straight), a 9 (three Nines, 2 left) or a 10/3/4 (two
  pair, 9 cards): about 35%.
- Best for winning the pot cheaply; poor for shedding snags (a straight carries
  none).

**Option B — throw the 7 and 5 ($18)**
- Gives up the straight.
- Triples the chance of three Nines (~5% → ~15%), which can carry **two snags**.
- Two pair or better about half the time (each fresh card can pair the board's
  10, 3 or 4, or the new cards can pair each other).
- Overall chance to win the pot stays roughly the same; snag-shedding chances
  rise sharply.

**The lesson:** with five snags aboard, you are not just playing for the pot.
Option B trades a cheap, snag-less straight draw for hands that can carry snags
overboard. With no snags, Option A would be the textbook play.

**Watch out:** the board already holds three clubs (10♣ 3♣ 4♣); a fourth club
on the River makes a flush possible for anyone holding just one club, so be wary
of loading snags then.

*What happened:* the captain threw the 7 and 5 and drew K♠ and J♦; the River
was the 8♣ (a fourth club).
