## 1. Debris faces

**Where:** `src/ui/portrait/faces.ts`, after Finlay, under the ids the table
already uses (`crate`, `sawyer`, `deadhead`, `driftPile`). All four are
`outline` heads, like Sandy and the ice sheet. Each has a `rest` pose so it
sits in character before any mood, and each is `dry` (no sweat beads on wood).

**Screenshot:** [`drafts-2026-11-17-faces.png`](drafts-2026-11-17-faces.png),
all four in neutral, confident and nervous, at 230 px. I rendered them on a
throwaway page, since the browser pane was hidden and its screenshots came
back stale. The page is deleted now. I didn’t sit at a debris table in the
dev scene, so the faces haven’t been seen at seat size in a real hand yet.
That check is still to do.

At the table a face’s mood comes from `Table.rivalMood`: nervous as its pile
drains, confident when it raises or wins. Debris have a pile of 30 and play
one hand, so mostly you’ll see the rest pose, then confident or nervous at the
verdict.

### Crate: hopeful, and soggy

A box of slats, a little askew, nail heads at the corners, a nail head for a
nose, and one top slat sprung and drooping off the right corner. A dark tide
mark along the bottom, two drips, and the river lapping at the bottom of the
frame. Its brows rest raised and a little worried (`browLift 2`,
`browTilt −0.6`) over a small smile. It’s eager, not scared.
- **Confident:** the smile widens, it leans into the frame. It looks pleased to be picked.
- **Nervous:** wide eyes, a wobbling mouth, a lean.
- **Notes:** this one reads best of the four. Tan on cream is clear even small.

### Sawyer: patient, sly

A trunk rising out of the water with a broken crown (three stubs of branches
poking out of the frame), long furrows of bark, a knot low down, and one twig
out to the side with two pale new leaves: spring, even for a sawyer. Umber
bark and soft eyes. It rests with heavy lids (`eyeOpen 0.6`), one brow cocked,
a smile mostly on one side, and a lean, as if caught mid-nod. It blinks
slowly, every 9½ seconds. Its tell (`shifty`) shows only when it’s confident.
- **Confident:** lids lower, the smirk deepens, the eyes slide aside now and then.
- **Nervous:** it straightens up, eyes open, the smile goes flat.
- **Notes:** it reads more as a *log* than a *tree*. If you want “a whole
  tree”, the next step is one bare branch arching out of the frame top-left,
  like the twig on the right but bigger.

### Deadhead: sullen, lurking

A log seen end-on, its rings and a check split in the end grain, and the river
running *over its crown*: a grey band of water across the top third, with pale
ripples. It’s a hand under the surface, looking up at you from under it.
Weed trails off its sides. It rests with cross brows, eyes rolled up from under
them, and a frown (`temper 0.5`: it doesn’t give much away). It glances aside
every seven seconds or so, always.
- **Confident:** the frown turns into a lopsided smirk. Just as cross.
- **Nervous:** brows lift a little, the mouth goes wavy.
- **Notes:** the water band reads well. At small sizes it may look like a
  hood, which is no bad thing for a lurker.

### Drift Pile: a jumble, cheerfully menacing

A barn door riding askew in the middle of the brush. Planks, two battens, and
a horseshoe still nailed on upside down (for somebody’s luck, not yours).
Sticks go every which way past the frame, brush is caught across the bottom
corners, there’s a leafy twig at each side, and a bird’s nest is snagged on
top. It rests with a big grin under cross brows: delighted to see you, and
heading for your wheel. Its brow twitches every six seconds.
- **Confident:** the grin gets bigger. Heaven help the wheel.
- **Nervous:** it keeps the grin, but the brows go up and the mouth wobbles. Rattled, still smiling.
- **Notes:** the busiest of the four. I tried a Z brace across the door
  first, but the diagonal cut the face in half, so it has two battens instead.

**Recommendation:** keep the Crate and the Deadhead as they are. Keep the
Drift Pile if it reads at seat size, and if not, take out the brush across
the bottom corners first. For the Sawyer, decide whether you want a log that
nods or a tree. If a tree, it needs one big bare branch.

## 2. The staple lines

### How the win line works now

`Journey.svelte:438` gives a table a `winLine` when any seat is an obstacle:
`river.gainedGround` if that obstacle is `steady`, `river.heldGround` if not.
`Table.svelte:908` then uses it in place of “You take the pot!”.

Every obstacle in the game is `steady`: the Sandbar, all four kinds of debris,
and the rapids chains. Only the tests have one that isn’t. So **every won hand
against anything that isn’t a captain says “She gains! A little further up.”**,
and “You hold your ground! The sand drifts away.” never shows in play. It was
written for the Sandbar and is stranded.

> **A likely bug, for a separate look.** `line()` (`Table.svelte:908`)
> returns `table.winLine` for *any* key when the seat is you, and that
> includes `hr.snagsTo`. So when a sawyer wins the hand with its snag played
> and the snag goes to you, the verdict should read “Sawyer takes the pot! She
> gains! A little further up.” where it should say “The snags go to you.” I
> haven’t seen it happen. This is from reading the code. The fix is to apply
> `winLine` only to `hr.wins` and `hr.byFolds`.

### To rotate them

This needs a small code change: `winLine?: string` becomes `winLines?: string[]`,
and the table picks one by hand number, plus the seed for one-hand tables.
Keys could be `river.won.<id>.1…n`, with `river.gainedGround` as the fallback.
I haven’t made that change. The lines below are written to replace “You take
the pot!”: short, a beat of action, an exclamation mark. Willie’s verdict
caption (“Clear of it, Captain!”) follows the debris hands, so these shouldn’t
say the same thing.

### The Sandbar (several hands a day; a won hand shifts her a little)

1. You hold your ground! The sand drifts away. *(the stranded line)*
2. She shifts! The bar gives a little.
3. The poles bite! She slides a hair.
4. A shovel’s worth of Sandy, back in the river!
5. She budges! The bar’s a little shallower. *(he hates being called shallow)*
6. That’s a few grains less of him!

### A crate (one hand; won, it’s aboard)

1. Hooked it! Over the rail, dripping.
2. Boat hook and a heave: it’s aboard!
3. Landed! Heavier than it looks.
4. Finders keepers! Up she comes.
5. Out of the river and onto the deck!

### Sawyer (nods up and down in the current)

1. You time the nod! Over it as it dips.
2. Past it between nods!
3. It bows, and you slip by!
4. Too slow on the nod, old tree!

### Deadhead (a hand under the surface)

1. You spot the swirl and swing wide!
2. Wide of it! It sulks off downstream.
3. It bobs up astern. Too late!
4. Missed you by a hand’s breadth!

### Drift pile (brush, branches and a barn door)

1. You thread the brush! Not a twig in the wheel.
2. Through a gap in the jumble!
3. Poles out, fended off, and past!
4. The barn door swings by, waving!

### The rapids chains (where the current line belongs)

1. She gains! A little further up. *(keep: it was right for here)*
2. A boat’s length gained!
3. She claws up another yard!
4. The wheel bites! Up she goes.

**And the losing side.** Losing to an obstacle says “Sawyer takes the pot!”,
which is odd for a tree. A matching `loseLine` per kind would fix it: “The
sand holds.” / “It’s got you!” / “The sawyer nods, smug.” / “The deadhead
won’t budge.” / “The pile drifts down on her!” / “The river won’t give.”

### What else repeats a lot

Ranked by how often a player hears it on one voyage, most first.

**The fork call** (`river.islandAhead`, `river.namedAhead`; `Journey.svelte:351`).
Said at every island, word for word.
- Island ahead, Captain! Port or starboard? *(keep)*
- Towhead off the bow, Captain! Which side? *(a towhead: a small island grown up in cottonwoods; Twain’s word)*
- Island coming up, Captain! Your call.
- {name} ahead, Captain! Which side of her?
- {name}, Captain! She’ll go either side. Port or starboard?

**Debris called from the bow** (`debris.ts`, `SIGHTED` and `crateTalk`). One
line per kind, every time.
- *Crate:* Crate in the water, Captain! Let’s fish it out. *(keep)* / Something
  floating, Captain! A crate, I think. Boat hook! / A crate off the bow,
  Captain, riding high. Dry inside, maybe! / Crate, Captain! Could be anything.
- *Sawyer:* Sawyer off the bow, Captain! She’ll need watching. *(keep)* /
  There’s a sawyer nodding at us, Captain. Don’t nod back. / Sawyer, Captain!
  Roots in the mud and all the time in the world.
- *Deadhead:* Deadhead, Captain! Just under the water, there. *(keep)* / See
  that swirl, Captain? Deadhead. / Something’s under there, Captain. Only the
  ripple gives it away.
- *Drift pile:* Drift pile, dead ahead! Mind the wheel, Captain. *(keep)* /
  Drift pile, Captain! Is that a barn door? / Half a fence and a barn door,
  coming our way, Captain!

**After the debris** (`hazardTalk`, `debrisRecord`).
- *Clear (Willie):* Clear of it, Captain! Not a scratch. *(keep)* / Past it,
  Captain! Paint and all. / Gone astern, Captain. Never laid a finger on us. /
  Clean past, Captain! I was holding my breath.
- *Struck (Gus):* That’s {snag}, Captain. We’ll live. *(keep)* / {Snag}. I’ll
  add it to the list, Captain. / That’ll be {snag}. Old Hildegard felt that one. /
  {Snag}, Captain. Could’ve been worse.
- *The log, clear:* Steered clear of a sawyer. *(keep)* / Passed a sawyer. It
  nodded. / Gave a deadhead a wide berth. / A drift pile went by to starboard,
  barn door and all.
- *The log, struck:* Struck a sawyer: Fouled Rudder. *(keep)* / A deadhead had
  a go at us: Sprung Plank. / Met a drift pile the hard way: Fouled Wheel.

**The deck game** (`crew.ts`). Gus opens every evening with “Anything broken,
Captain, lay it on the table. We’ll see to it.” Nine flop quips, one per hand,
three hands a night, so they come round again on the fourth evening. The
fixing lines repeat once per snag.
- *Gus’s opener:* (keep) / Show us what’s broke, Captain. / Cards and repairs,
  Captain. Lay down what ails her. / Lay it out, Captain. Hildegard and I are listening.
- *Gus fixing:* I think I can fix {snag}, Captain. *(keep)* / {Snag}? Give me
  an hour and a wrench. / I’ve mended worse than {snag}, Captain.
- *Willie fixing:* Leave that one with me, Captain! *(keep)* / I’ll see to it
  first thing, Captain! / Mine, Captain! I know just the knot.
- The flop quips want three or four more for the second week. Lars’s words
  already change after a week, so his are the cheapest to add.
- The beans are still in `crew.ts` on this branch (a NAG and a deck quip). I
  left them alone and kept them out of everything here.

**Recommendation:** hook the stranded Sandbar line back up first. It’s already
written and costs one condition. Then do per-kind win lines with rotation
(three or four each), and the fork call’s alternatives, since those come up most
often. Look at the `snagsTo` bug before any of it. The debris and deck-game
alternatives can wait until the crate contents (below) land, since they touch
the same code.

## 3. What’s in the crates

A crate is worth its ante: $2 for the first on a leg, a dollar more for each
one met on it since, so mostly $2 to $6. I’ve grouped the finds in three tiers
so the joke matches the money: a crate worth $2 shouldn’t be a ship’s bell.
Pick within the tier from the debris spot’s hash, like `debrisAt`, so a reload
finds the same thing, and skip finds already made this voyage until the tier
runs out. Saves are disposable, so a new field means a new save key.

Each entry has what Willie says when the hand is won, then the log line.

### Small finds ($2–3)

1. **Clothes-pegs.** “Clothes-pegs, Captain! Hundreds of them. We’re set for life.” · *Fished out a crate of clothes-pegs.*
2. **Corncob pipes, a gross.** “Corncob pipes, Captain, a gross of them! Gus has already picked one.” · *A gross of corncob pipes. Gus took the best.*
3. **Hymnals.** “Hymnals, Captain. Only a little damp. Lars is singing from one already.” · *A crate of hymnals. Lars approves.*
4. **Horseshoes.** “Horseshoes, Captain! Two dozen. That’s a lot of luck.” · *Two dozen horseshoes. Luck, by the pound.*
5. **Bicycle bells.** “Bicycle bells, Captain. No bicycles. Just the bells.” · *A crate of bicycle bells, ringing.*
6. **Seed packets.** “Seed packets, Captain, enough for a whole county’s gardens!” · *Seed packets, dry in their tins.*
7. **Sheet music.** “Sheet music, Captain! Waltzes, mostly. Damp, but you can still read the notes.” · *A crate of sheet music. Lars hums along.*
8. **Doorknobs.** “Doorknobs, Captain. Porcelain ones. All we need now is doors.” · *Fished out a crate of doorknobs. No doors.*
9. **Horehound candy.** “Horehound drops, Captain! Good for the throat, Lars says. In his way.” · *A crate of horehound candy, a little stuck together.*

### Middling ($4–5)

10. **Mason jars.** “Mason jars, Captain, lids and all! Somebody meant to put up a lot of peaches.” · *A crate of Mason jars. Not one cracked.*

12. **Skillets.** “Cast-iron skillets, Captain! Now all we need is a stove.” · *A crate of skillets. A stove would be nice.*
13. **Mouth organs.** “Mouth organs, Captain! Two dozen, and Lars has already found the one he likes.” · *Two dozen mouth organs. Lars kept one.*
14. **Straw boaters.** “Straw boaters, Captain! A little soggy at the brim. Very smart.” · *A crate of boaters. The crew look very smart.*
15. **Spectacles.** “Spectacles, Captain, a whole box. Everything’s much clearer now.” · *A box of spectacles. Gus tried on six pairs.*
16. **Mail-order catalogues.** “Catalogues, Captain! Reading for a year, and you can’t afford a page of it.” · *A crate of mail-order catalogues.*
17. **Oranges.** “Oranges, Captain! From Florida, it says. A bit bruised, and very sweet.” · *A crate of oranges. Ate two.*
18. **Nails.** “Nails, Captain, a whole keg! Gus has stopped grumbling for a minute.” · *A keg of nails. Gus, briefly happy.*
19. **Coffee, in tins.** “Coffee, Captain, roasted and sealed! Gus has gone quiet. That’s how you know.” · *Tins of coffee. A quiet engine room.*

### Good finds ($6 and up)

20. **Pearl buttons from Muscatine.** “Pearl buttons, Captain, off the Muscatine works! Sewn on cards, every one.” · *Pearl buttons, carded, from home.* *(Muscatine’s button works cut them from river mussel shells, a nice touch for chapter one)*
21. **Dollar watches.** “Pocket watches, Captain, a dozen, and every one still ticking!” · *A dozen dollar watches, all going.*
22. **Window glass.** “Window glass, Captain, and not a crack in it!” · *Window glass, whole. A small miracle.*
23. **A ship’s bell.** “A ship’s bell, Captain! Brass. Gus is polishing it already.” · *A brass bell, off some other boat.*
24. **Phonograph cylinders.** “Phonograph cylinders, Captain! Brass band music, it says on the box.” · *A crate of cylinders. No phonograph.*
25. **A sewing machine.** “A sewing machine, Captain! The head of one, anyway. The rest is somebody else’s problem.” · *Half a sewing machine. The good half.*
26. **Calico, in oilcloth.** “Calico, Captain, bolts of it, dry in oilcloth! Someone packed for the river.” · *Bolts of calico, bone dry.*

### Lost: nothing worth having (Gus, in the spirit of soggy cotton)

1. It’s just soggy cotton, boys. *(keep)*
2. Sawdust. The packing for something that isn’t here any more.
3. Straw, boys, and a frog who’d like to be left alone.
4. Somebody’s laundry, boys. Put it back gently.
5. Mud. A whole crate of genuine Mississippi mud.
6. Papers, boys. Somebody’s accounts. Pulp now. Their debts are forgiven.
7. Empty bottles. Somebody had a nice evening.
8. It’s empty, boys. Somebody beat us to it.
9. Lard. Gone off. Close the lid, boys, close the lid.
10. Wet sawdust and a note that says “FRAGILE.”

Log lines for a loss, in the same form as now: *Fished out a crate. Soggy
cotton.* / *…Sawdust.* / *…Straw and a frog.* / *…Somebody’s laundry.* /
*…Mud.* / and so on, the one word or two from each line above.

**Recommendation:** take the three tiers with about eight finds each, and keep
the Muscatine buttons whatever else goes. They’re local, period and warm.
Show the find in Willie’s verdict and in the log, with the dollar amount
still there (“Pearl buttons, Captain, off the Muscatine works! Worth $6 at the
Mercantile.”), so the money stays readable. Rotate the lost lines without
tiers. The frog (3) and the forgiven debts (6) are the best of them.
