# Playtest, chapter one (2026-11-17)

A bot played chapter one, from Bill's handover at Muscatine to the top of the rapids at LeClaire
(where the story says *To be continued*), 1,000 times: 250 seeds each for four captains. Another 750
voyages tried variants. That first pass changed nothing in the game. The sections after "Update"
measure the river **as it was** before the first stretch was reworked the same evening; the Update
says what changed and what it did.

## Update, the same evening: the first stretch reworked

**What changed** (agreed in the session):

- **Willie types through anything.** The river no longer keeps quiet while he types, or for 12 s
  after. Debris comes whenever the stream finds room. The bot can still play the old rule:
  `PLAYTEST_TYPING=1`.
- **A crate first, always, out of Muscatine.** The stretch's `fills: ['crate']` is a set piece, met
  before the river's own coin flips. Once met it is gone from the rest of the stretch.
- **What Willie writes, in order:**
  1. As she backs out: *The first day of spring.*
  2. As the wheel turns ahead: *Cargo run for Dubuque…*
  3. After the first crate:
     - won: what was in it, with its worth (a find from the small tier of "What's in the crates");
     - lost: far too much read into it. Three new drafts, for review, in `FIRST_CRATE` (log.ts).
  4. After the second piece: its usual line, then the dock fee, Captain against harbour master. The
     two lines are kept, and the coal lines are gone. If no second piece comes, the dock fee is
     written before The Sandbar.
  5. Then the river's own pieces, and The Sandbar as it was.
- **A crate in the starboard passage round each island, once The Sandbar is behind her**
  (`laneCrates: { side: 'starboard', afterMain: true }`). It is drawn in that passage so it can be
  spotted, and met by steering that way. It doesn't count with the leg's crates, so its ante doesn't
  climb. **At Horse Island the starboard passage is the main channel** (the Illinois-bank
  "Mississippi River", 518 m), so everyone who doesn't steer meets it. Putting it in the chute
  instead is a one-word change: `side: 'port'`.
- **The bot** plays all of this, and there is a fifth captain, **savvy**. Savvy is the careful one,
  but plays the harbour master and, while saving up, runs coal: the depot's coal, at about a dozen
  dollars lost a bunker, sold at the Mercantile for $8 a load.

**What it does** (250 seeds each):

| persona | pieces before / after The Sandbar | strength at The Sandbar (p10 / p50) | Sandbar cleared on day 1 | at the Tri-Cities with 0 strength | salvage $ at the Tri-Cities (p50 / p90) | chapter done (p50 / p90) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| careful | 3.0 / 1.8 | 0 / 9 | 34% (was 42%) | **36%** (was 3%) | 5 / 11 | 15 / 27 days |
| loose | 2.9 / 1.7 | 0 / 8 | 29% | 44% | 5 / 12 | 20 / 34 |
| novice | 3.0 / 1.8 | 7 / 12 | 26% | 32% | 3 / 9 | 20 / 34 |
| reckless | 2.6 / 1.7 | 0 / 1 | 20% | 49% | 5 / 11 | – (99% time out) |
| **savvy** | 3.0 / 1.8 | 0 / 9 | 34% | 36% | 5 / 11 | **13 / 25** |

- **The sequence plays as designed.** On every seed there is the crate at t ≈ 0.06, a piece at
  about 0.15 and another at about 0.25. Then the two islands, and The Sandbar at 0.396. After it,
  one or two more pieces, and the Horse Island crate (met on 100% of runs, since it's the main
  channel).
- **But the crew can't afford it.** About five crate or hazard hands in a day, at about 3 strength
  each, with each crate's ante a dollar more than the last on the leg (2, 3, 4, 5…). The crew arrive at
  The Sandbar with half their strength, and a third of careful captains reach the Tri-Cities with
  none. That breaks *"the crew shouldn't run out of strength from debris alone"*.
- **Re-simulated fixes** (day one only, 200 careful seeds each):

  | change | Sandbar cleared on day 1 | strength at The Sandbar (p10 / p50) | at the Tri-Cities with 0 strength | pieces before / after The Sandbar |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | as built now | 33% | 0 / 9 | 36% | 3.0 / 1.8 |
  | no debris called at strength ≤ 6 | 34% | 1 / 9 | 12% | 2.8 / 1.3 |
  | crates' ante stays at $2 | 38% | 3 / 10 | 20% | 3.0 / 1.9 |
  | **both** | **39%** | **3 / 10** | **0%** | 2.8 / 1.5 |
  | both, floor at 9 | 38% | 5 / 10 | 1% | 2.6 / 1.2 |

  **Recommendation: both.** Don't call debris with the crew at 6 or below (the UI now checks
  `strength > 0`), and keep the crate's ante at $2 on a leg. Both are one-line changes, and a lost
  crate is then never worth more than about 6 strength. With them, nobody reaches the Tri-Cities
  empty, The Sandbar is back near where it was, and the stretch still has its three pieces before
  The Sandbar on nearly every run. The bot has both as options (`PLAYTEST_FLOOR=6`,
  `PLAYTEST_FLAT_ANTE=1`).
- **Savvy is the quickest captain, but not 3 days.** Boiler on day 3 (p90 5), first day at the
  Games on day 7, first noon won on day 11, done on day 13. Selling coal brings the boiler forward
  by 2 days. The Games' lottery (about one day in five, for the careful AI at the table) still
  sets the pace after that. A human who plays the Games better than the `careful` AI will be much
  quicker, and the bot can't tell how much.

## The first pass

- **The bot:** `src/story/sim/playtest.ts` (`playVoyage(seed, persona)`). It drives the real voyage
  functions and adds what `Journey.svelte` adds on top of them: the event stream at `legSeconds` 420,
  the debris it calls, the islands' busy stretches, Willie's typing (the river stays quiet while he types
  and for 12 s after), gathering way, and creeping on during a hand. It writes the UI's log lines (the
  departure entries, the dock-fee line, Bill's handover, island and debris records), because those
  lines set the timing (see 2).
- **The runner:** `npm run sim -- scripts/playtest.sim.ts` (env: `PLAYTEST_SEEDS`, `PLAYTEST_FROM`,
  `PLAYTEST_PERSONAS`, `PLAYTEST_OUT`, `PLAYTEST_DAYS`, `PLAYTEST_TYPING=1` for the river's old
  quiet while Willie typed, `PLAYTEST_TUNE` for encounter piles and hands, `PLAYTEST_PRICE` for shop
  prices, `PLAYTEST_FLOOR` and `PLAYTEST_FLAT_ANTE` for the debris fixes in the Update). The tables: `node scripts/playtest-summary.mjs runs*.json`.
  The Sandbar on its own: `npm run sim -- scripts/sandbar-tuning.sim.ts`. None of these is in the
  default `npx vitest run`. One voyage takes 2 to 8 s, so 250 seeds is about 20 minutes on one core
  (run shards in parallel).
- **Deterministic:** the same seed and persona always give the same run. Rerunning one seed
  reproduces it.

## The captains

| persona | at the table | the dock fee | the depot | goes for the rapids at | shops | evenings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **careful** | `careful` AI | plays for it | plays to win the first time, then throws the hands (works out the foreman) | strength 34 (Gus's figure) | boiler, then fireman and food the day before | Bettendorf if the purse is ≥ $30, else at anchor |
| **loose** | `loose` | plays for it | the same | 26 | the same | Bettendorf at ≥ $15; hires Gaylord |
| **novice** | `stubborn`, and stands pat on every draw | pays it | always plays to win | any strength | the same as careful (follows Gus) | always at anchor |
| **reckless** | `swashbuckler` | plays for it | plays to win | any strength | never | Bettendorf every night; takes Gaylord's loan |

All of them anchor until Willie has heard of the pilot, anchor while carrying a trouble, fill the
bunker at the depot only when the climb is near, and otherwise work: cattle or timber when Willie has
found the job, the mail run when not. At a failed push they always push again while there is coal.

**Hand times on the river (APPROX):** 25 to 60 s, now and then 1.5 to 4 minutes (careful, novice), or
12 to 32 s (loose, reckless). A hand at a landing doesn't touch the stream.

## The headlines

1. **Chapter one takes a careful captain 16 days (median; 10% take 29 or more).** The design wants
   *"a day or two at the Tri-Cities"* and 2 to 2½ weeks for the **whole voyage**. The time goes to
   saving for the boiler (bought on day 5), then saving for the depot, the fireman and food (first
   day at the Games, day 10), then the Games' lottery (first noon win, day 14), then the climb.
   Details in 5.
2. **The river has exactly one piece of debris in chapter one, for every captain, on every seed, and
   none before The Sandbar.** Willie's departure entries keep the river quiet until the first island's
   warning. After that, the islands and The Sandbar's window leave no room. After The Sandbar there is
   room for exactly one piece, at t ≈ 0.61, between Horse Island and the Tri-Cities. Details in 2.
3. **Strength barely decides The Sandbar's first day.** A careful captain clears it on 44% of tries
   with the crew's 18, and 39% with 9. The pile and the number of hands decide it. This matters for
   the band design, which says *"the band's strength is what turns it"*. Details in 1.
4. **Nothing is broken.** In 1,750 voyages there were no thrown errors, no softlocks and no negative
   purse, coal or strength. Every run that didn't finish in 40 days was still playing, losing at the
   Games day after day. One rule isn't enforced: see "Broken, or nearly".
5. **Queuing early is a trap with no exit.** 36 timed-out runs went to the Games at strength 18 and
   never saved for the boiler (see "Broken, or nearly").
6. **Money is tight to the boiler, then it stops mattering.** 92% of careful runs hit $0 at some
   point, and the median purse on day 15 is $3. But from the boiler on, the odd job stakes the
   morning entry whenever the purse is under $5, so being broke costs nothing at the Games. The
   story intends that (*"Losing days pass in odd jobs off screen, enough to fund the next entry"*).
   The effect is that from about day 10 the purse is close to irrelevant.

## 1. The Sandbar

Day 1 is the try on the first evening; day 2 is the next morning against what's left.

| persona | cleared day 1 | day 2 | day 3 | day 4+ | nights stuck (mean) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| careful | **42%** | 50% | 7% | 1% | 0.68 |
| loose | 40% | 45% | 12% | 3% | 0.79 |
| novice | 24% | 54% | 18% | 4% | 1.03 |
| reckless | 40% | 34% | 17% | 9% | 0.98 |

So a careful captain is stranded on the first day 58% of the time.

**Against the intent.** The docs disagree with each other here:

- `docs/story.md` (first stretch, as built) says it is cleared one time in four on day one.
- `sandy.ts` says two times in five (an older 80-voyage sim).
- "The house band" says *"Day one is hopeless"*.
- The brief I was given says it should strand a careful player about one time in two to four.

The measured 42% matches `sandy.ts`, sits at the generous end of "one in two to four", and is far
from "hopeless" or "one in four". **This needs a decision on which intent wins.** Re-simulated
options for that decision are below.

**Strength hardly matters** (`scripts/sandbar-tuning.sim.ts`, The Sandbar alone, 400 tries each,
first day):

| strength brought | careful | loose | stubborn | swashbuckler |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | 44% | 44% | 32% | 44% |
| 15 | 44% | 44% | 32% | 45% |
| 12 | 43% | 41% | 31% | 37% |
| 9 | 39% | 36% | 30% | 32% |

Four hands at antes of 2 and 3 can't take 20 from the pile unless the hands go your way. The crew
almost never runs out first. Debris before The Sandbar would barely matter even if there were some.
A band that adds **strength** would not turn it.

**Tuning, re-simulated** (careful, first day):

| change | cleared day 1 | by day 2 | effect on the chapter's days |
|---|---|---|---|
| as built (pile 20, 4 hands) | 44% (alone) / 42% (whole voyage) | 92% | – |
| pile 24 | 26% / **23%** | 91% | none measurable (15 vs 16) |
| pile 28 | 15% | – | – |
| 3 hands | 20% | – | – |
| 5 hands | 59% | – | – |
| ante from 1 (`antePast: 0`) | 18% | – | – |

- **For "one time in four" (story.md):** pile 24. That gives 23% on day one, and by day two it is
  still cleared 91% of the time, because the morning brings 8 hands.
- **For "strand one time in two to four":** 5 hands. That gives 59% cleared, so 41% stranded.
- **For "day one hopeless, the band turns it":** the band must bring **hands or a volunteer**, not
  strength. A band snag with `helps: { from: 1, to: 8 }` (snags.ts) gives back every pot lost while
  they play, which is the mechanic built for exactly this. Alternatively give Sandy a pile he can't
  lose in 4 hands (28 or more) and let the band's morning add the hands.

## 2. Debris on the river

| persona | met per voyage (p10 / p50 / p90) | before / after The Sandbar | strength lost per piece | won: crate / sawyer / deadhead / drift pile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| careful | 1 / 1 / 1 | 0 / 1 | 2.4 | 59% / 51% / 59% / 55% |
| loose | 1 / 1 / 1 | 0 / 1 | 2.8 | 56% / 43% / 51% / 60% |
| novice | 1 / 1 / 1 | 0 / 1 | 1.8 | 40% / 45% / 46% / 41% |
| reckless | 1 / 1 / 1 | 0 / 1 | 4.1 | 56% / 59% / 79% / 61% |

**Why only one.** Leaving Muscatine, Willie types three entries one after another:

| entry | typing time (APPROX) |
|---|---|
| backing out | ≈ 17 s |
| turning | ≈ 42 s |
| the dock fee and the coal | ≈ 61 to 78 s, depending on how the fee went |

If the fee was **paid**, his line about Bill's handover is still in the queue too, because the five
harbour hands that would have given him time weren't played. The river stays quiet until 12 s after
the last entry. By then she is about 25% up the leg, and the first island's warning is due at
t ≈ 0.311. A debris hand needs 20 s of room (lead, a crawl's worth of hand, the margin), and it is
a matter of seconds. An earlier pass of the bot, which didn't write the dock-fee and handover lines,
found that room at t ≈ 0.25 for every captain who played for the fee. Then come two islands back to
back. As she clears the second she is already in The Sandbar's window with no room left for a fill,
so The Sandbar comes there, every time: t = 0.3962 on all 1,000 seeds, well before the window's
middle at 0.418. After The Sandbar: a 35 s rest, Horse Island, its log line, and then room for
exactly one piece before the Tri-Cities, at t = 0.610 to 0.613.

**Without the typing** (`PLAYTEST_TYPING=0`, 150 seeds each): 3 pieces before The Sandbar and 1
after, every time. The careful captain then reaches The Sandbar with strength 9 (median), 15% reach
the Tri-Cities at 0 strength, and 122 debris calls found the crew with no strength. The first-day
clear rate falls from 42% to 37%. **The typewriter is load-bearing:** speed it up, cut an entry, or
let it skip while a table is up, and the first stretch roughly quadruples its debris and drains the
crew.

**Against the intent** (*"the crew shouldn't run out of strength from debris alone"*): as built it
doesn't. 3% of careful captains and 16% of reckless ones reach the Tri-Cities at 0, and that comes
from The Sandbar more than the one piece. Without the typing it would fail that test.

**Salvage is worth almost nothing:** $0.55 per voyage on average (careful). It sells at $1 each, at
the Mercantile, behind a $3 docking fee. The crate is a coin flip for $2 that nobody can spend.

**Recommendations:**

- If debris is meant to be a regular beat, not a one-off: stop counting **typing** as a reason for
  quiet on the river, and keep only the 12 s after an entry. Or let debris come during a long entry.
  Then re-tune the debris hand's cost, which as measured would empty the crew before The Sandbar. A
  middle way: allow at most one piece before The Sandbar (`main` could reserve its rest).
- Make the crate worth carrying. Either sell salvage at the morning council (no docking fee), or start
  the crate's ante at $3 to $4.

## 4. Money

Median purse at dawn (the number of runs still playing on that day in brackets):

| persona | day 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 10 | 15 | 20 | ever at $0 | odd jobs per voyage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| careful | 50 | 14 | 14 | 25 | 21 | 12 | 3 (145) | 2 (85) | 92% | 6.5 |
| loose | 50 | 38 | 27 | 17 | 17 | 13 | 4 (205) | 3 (164) | 97% | 9.4 |
| novice | 50 | 7 | 1 | 25 | 25 | 23 | 14 (178) | 13 (128) | 94% | 5.9 |
| reckless | 50 | 24 | 20 | 15 | 18 | 19 | 14 (250) | 16 (248) | 99% | 10.2 |

**What each purse table does**, in dollars per game (mean, then p10 / p50 / p90):

| table | careful | loose | reckless |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bettendorf (trips) | −1.3 (−11 / −4 / +15) | −4.0 | −3.0 |
| the depot | −8.7 (−17 / −13 / +5) | −10.2 | −4.9 |
| the Games, morning ($5 in) | +9.9 | +9.6 | +5.1 |
| the Games, noon ($10 in) | +4.2 (−10 / −10 / +30) | +1.0 | −2.9 |

- **Income** is the jobs: cattle or timber $10 to $14 once per night at anchor, the mail run $9.
  Each burns a load of coal. **Outgo** is wages of $6 a day, the depot's dozen dollars, and the
  Mercantile's $3 door fee.
- **A careful captain nets about $10 to $15 a day.** The boiler at $40 takes until day 5.
- **Bettendorf is a slow leak for everyone.** For a loose captain it is the difference between
  finishing (78%) and not: the loose runs that time out mostly sat at Bettendorf every night at $15
  or more and never saved for the fireman.

**Against the intent** (*"money should be tight but not hopeless"*):

- Tight, yes. $0 is normal from about day 12.
- Hopeless, never: the odd job means nobody is ever stuck.
- What it isn't is *interesting* after the boiler. With the Games free to enter when broke, the purse
  no longer decides anything.

## 5. Where the days go

| persona | boiler bought | first day at the Games | first noon won | done (p50 / p90) | done within 40 days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| careful | day 5 (p90 8) | 10 (14) | 14 (24) | **16 / 29** | 98% |
| loose | 6 (12) | 11 (18) | 19 (31) | 21 / 34 | 78% |
| novice | 9 (24) | 10 (24) | 12 (24) | 17 / 31 | 79% |
| reckless | never | 22 (34) | 27 (38) | 19 / 23 | 1% |

How the Games and the climb went:

| persona | morning won | noon won | days at the Games (p50 / p90) | Moline / Campbell's / Long Reach, per push | climbs begun (mean) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| careful | 46% | 41% | 6 / 17 | 93% / 69% / **30%** | 1.41 |
| loose | 40% | 32% | 8 / 24 | 84% / 56% / 25% | 1.43 |
| novice | 53% | 45% | 5 / 17 | 87% / 60% / 18% | 1.68 |
| reckless (strength 18) | 28% | 20% | 5 / 24 | 26% / 14% / 4% | 0.47 |

- **A day at the Games is won about one time in five** (46% × 41% for careful). That is the queue the
  story wants (*"stuck here a week, then that's a week of poker"*), and it gives the median of 6
  days.
- **The climb at 38 strength** (boiler, fireman, plus food: 42) succeeds about 70% of the time on the
  first try. That matches story.md's 58% at 34 and 84% at 44.
- **Without upgrades it's a wall.** Strength 18 clears the Long Reach on 4% of pushes. That is Gus's
  point, made by the numbers.
- **The novice does surprisingly well at the Games** (53% / 45%). Standing pat is strong in
  multi-way tournament tables against these AIs. Worth a look at the AIs, not the Games.
- **Money is a smaller lever than the Games.** Re-simulated with the boiler at $30 (150 careful
  seeds): boiler on day 4, first Games on day 8, done on day 14 (p90 28), down from 16. With the
  Long Reach at 45 (150 seeds): it clears on 46% of pushes and 1.12 climbs are begun, but the
  chapter is done on day 15, down from 16.

**Recommendations, to bring chapter one towards "a day or two at the Tri-Cities":**

1. **Shorten the saving.** Each $6 a day of wages that isn't paid, or $10 off the boiler, is about a
   day. Options: let the first depot game be the real coal source, so the bunker is full on arrival
   (an estimate from the milestones, not simulated: about 4 days). Or pay the cattle job $20. Or put
   the boiler at $30 (re-simulated: −2 days).
2. **Thin the Games' lottery.** For example, after two losing days the morning table admits a
   captain who came second (the runner-up rule), which roughly doubles the morning odds. Or bring in
   Lindy Huffer (the "highest bidder" pilot already in story.md) from about day 6. Not simulated.
   Expected: the 6-day queue down to 2 or 3.
3. **Leave the climb alone.** It does what the doc says. If anything, the Long Reach could go down to
   about 50 for a captain who has done everything right (45 was re-simulated: 46% a push, and a day
   saved), but that isn't where the days go.

## Broken, or nearly

- **The depot's `max` isn't enforced.** The goal is `{ type: 'lose', min: 12, max: 25 }`, and the
  comment says *"Lose between min and max"*, but `handDone` only checks `min`. 134 of the reckless
  captain's 5,794 depot games lost more than $25. Repro: reckless seed 7 loses $33, novice seed 63
  loses $32, loose seed 15 loses $28 (`playVoyage(7, PERSONAS.reckless)`, see `depotCost`). Either
  enforce it (fail, or end, past `max`) or drop it from the type.
- **The depot always gives the coal**, whatever you do. Novices playing to win got it in 99% of 2,875
  games: over 8 hands, $12 lost at showdowns happens anyway. The foreman's game, written as a
  riddle, is no riddle. If it should be: make `min` 12 net, not 12 gross, or cut it to 5 hands.
- **Gaylord's loan is free money in this slice.** $50, and no repayment is built (`owesGaylord` only
  changes what he offers). Known, presumably; worth a flag before anyone balances around it.
- **A pre-existing knife-edge:** whether a debris piece fits before the first island depends on
  seconds of typing (2). It's fine as built, but it will flip with any change to a log entry's
  length, the departure animation, or `restSeconds`.
- **No softlocks, but one trap.** 114 non-reckless runs timed out at 40 days. Their strength at the
  end:
  - **54 at 38 and 24 at 24:** at the Games every morning, with the coal, losing.
  - **36 at 18:** they went to the Games (or sat at Bettendorf) before buying the boiler, and never
    saved the $48 for it. **A day at the Games earns nothing.** The odd job covers the entry, and a
    lost morning ends the day, so a captain who queues at 18 strength can stay there indefinitely,
    stuck on 4% Long Reach pushes. Repro: loose seed 17 sat at Bettendorf most nights and ended with
    $63 and no boiler. Most novice timeouts follow the same pattern.
  - Whether that's River Code or a trap worth a nudge (Gus refusing to queue below 34, as story.md
    half-says: *"Below 34 strength, he won't let it drop"*) is a design call.

## What the bot can't judge

- **Fun, and pacing feel.** 16 days on the calendar is not 16 days of play. A day at the hub is a few
  minutes, and the Games are the best tables in the chapter. Whether a week at the Tri-Cities feels
  like a grind or like living there (*"a place to live a while"*) needs people.
- **Human play.** The bot reads no talk and never bluffs. It doesn't use the Mercantile's coal
  trade (selling at $8 while it's short), and never turns back from a push. A thinking player
  probably saves a day or two on the money and the depot. Some won't work out the depot at all.
- **Real hand times.** Hand seconds are a guess. On the river they barely matter, because the room
  for debris is set by the islands and the typing, not by how quick the player is. At The Sandbar
  and the tables they don't matter at all.
- **The typing** is TypedLine's average speed, not its exact random delays. The knife-edge in 2 could
  go either way for a real player, but within seconds, not pieces.
- **Steering round the islands.** The bot takes the main passage. Nothing is in the passages, so it
  makes no difference.
